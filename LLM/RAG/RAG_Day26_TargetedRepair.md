## Step 2 — Classify the Failure Type (This Determines Your Repair Strategy)

For each row above, classify it into one of three buckets — the fix is different for each:

### Bucket A: "I genuinely didn't know this"
**Symptom:** blank stare, no idea where to even start.
**Repair strategy:** go back to the specific day's file, re-read the Deep Dive section fully, then close the file and try to re-derive the answer from scratch without looking. Don't move on until you can produce the answer unprompted.

### Bucket B: "I knew it existed but couldn't produce the mechanism/formula under time pressure"
**Symptom:** you recognized the concept name but couldn't explain *how* or compute the actual number.
**Repair strategy:** this is usually a fluency problem, not a knowledge gap — redo the specific worked numerical example from that day's file by hand, from memory, timing yourself. Repeat until you can do it in under 90 seconds without hesitation.

### Bucket C: "I knew the individual pieces but couldn't synthesize them under pressure"
**Symptom:** you could probably have answered each sub-part separately, but combining them into one coherent answer (especially Part 3/4-style questions) fell apart.
**Repair strategy:** this is the hardest bucket to fix with passive review — re-read the relevant cross-week synthesis questions from Days 6, 12, 18, 21, 24, then practice explaining the connection **out loud, in full sentences**, not just nodding along when you read the answer. Synthesis is a speaking skill, not a recognition skill.

## Step 4 — The One Pattern Worth Checking For

Before doing individual repairs, look at your full list of misses and ask: **is there one recurring theme across multiple misses**, rather than a scattered set of unrelated gaps?

Common patterns worth specifically checking for, based on what typically shows up at this stage of prep:

- **"I'm weak on all calculation questions, regardless of topic"** → this isn't a knowledge gap, it's a fluency-under-time-pressure gap. Repair: redo 10-15 worked calculations from across the whole curriculum (BM25, RRF, nDCG, capacity planning, cost modeling) back-to-back, timed, as a dedicated drill — not spread across different days' review sessions.
- **"I'm weak specifically on Generation-week concepts (Days 13-17)"** → this was covered furthest from today; likely a spaced-repetition decay issue, not a true gap. Repair: a fast full re-read of Days 13-17's cheat sheets (just the cheat sheets, not full re-reads) plus Day 18's review questions again.
- **"I can explain individual concepts but freeze on synthesis/diagnostic questions"** → this is Bucket C at scale, and it's the most dangerous pattern to carry into Mock #2 and the real interview, since live interviews are almost entirely synthesis-style follow-up questions, not isolated definitions. Repair: prioritize this over everything else — redo every cross-week synthesis question (Days 6, 12, 18, 21, 24 Section F/C questions) out loud today.
- **"I'm weak specifically on the Apple-specific content (Day 23)"** → this is genuinely newer, less-reinforced material with only one prior exposure. Repair: a full re-read of Day 23, then attempt Day 24's Section C questions again from scratch.

## Step 5 — Confidence Check Before Moving On

Before considering today's repair complete, try this test: pick **three** of your originally-missed questions at random and answer them again, out loud, without looking at anything. If you can produce a confident, complete answer for all three, you're ready to move forward. If not, spend more time on that specific bucket before Day 27's second mock interview — don't move on based on time elapsed rather than actual readiness.

---

## 🧠 A Note on What NOT to Do Today

- ❌ Don't re-read every single day's file front to back "just to be safe" — that's exactly the undirected review this targeted approach is designed to replace, and at this stage in prep it's a worse use of limited time than focused repair.
- ❌ Don't just re-read the answer to a missed question and feel satisfied that it "makes sense now" — recognition is not the same as recall under pressure. Always close the file and try to reproduce the answer unprompted before checking the box.
- ❌ Don't skip Step 4's pattern-check — an unstructured list of 8 individual misses is much less useful than recognizing "6 of these 8 are actually the same underlying gap."
